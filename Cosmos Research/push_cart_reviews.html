Buyer's Guide

3-Wheel vs 4-Wheel
Three-wheel carts pivot easier and feel more athletic; four-wheel carts are more stable on slopes. For most walking golfers, three wheels is the sweet spot.
Fold Matters Most
You'll fold and unfold this thing 100+ times a year. Two-step or flat-fold designs save your back and your trunk.
Brake Quality
A foot brake should engage cleanly and never drift. Test it on any slope before committing to a cart you'll keep.
Skip the Gimmicks
Bluetooth speakers, GPS mounts, USB ports — nice to have, but a wobbly frame ruins them all. Buy the chassis first, accessorize second.
Bag Compatibility
Confirm your stand or cart bag fits the upper and lower brackets. Most major brands play nice, but oversized tour bags can be a squeeze.
Wheel Size
Larger wheels handle wet, sandy, or hilly courses far better. If you walk Florida or links-style turf, prioritize bigger wheels.

FAQ

Is a push cart actually better than carrying?
For most golfers, yes. Studies and our own testing show pushing burns nearly the same calories as carrying without the back fatigue, and your scores typically improve over the back nine because you're fresher.
Three wheels or four?
Three is more maneuverable and lighter. Four is more stable when stopped on a slope. Both of our top picks are three-wheel for a reason — most courses don't need the extra stability.
How long should a quality push cart last?
A well-built cart from a trusted brand should last 5-10+ years of regular play. The Bag Boy in particular has a reputation for outlasting the bags people put on it.
Are these carts allowed on all courses?
Yes, push carts are permitted at virtually every public and private course. A few high-end private clubs restrict them on certain days — always check ahead if you're a guest.
